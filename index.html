Section 1: Background, Policy Rationale & Evolution (መግቢያ እና መነሻዎች)

Q1 • Background
How does the April 2024 Policy classify the historical stages (generations) of Ethiopia’s health policy evolution, and what were the primary achievements of the first three generations?

Answer: The policy classifies healthcare evolution into four distinct generations:

  • 1st Generation (1960s E.C.): Focused on infectious disease eradication campaigns, notably smallpox eradication and control of major epidemic threats.
  • 2nd Generation (Mid-1970s E.C.): Initiated formal Primary Health Care (PHC) strategies and launched the Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI).
  • 3rd Generation (1993 / 1986 E.C.): Formulated the 1993 Health Policy, establishing a 3-tier delivery system, decentralized administration, and launching the Health Extension Program (HEP), which expanded basic care to rural communities.
Q2 • Rationale
What major demographic, epidemiological, socio-economic, and health system challenges necessitated replacing the 1993 (1986 E.C.) Health Policy?

Answer: Key drivers for policy revision included:

  • Triple Burden of Disease: High NCD prevalence (cardiovascular, cancer, diabetes) alongside persisting communicable diseases and rising trauma/injuries.
  • High Out-of-Pocket Expense: Severe financial hardship facing households accessing secondary and tertiary care.
  • Health Security Threats: Vulnerabilities highlighted by COVID-19, public health emergencies, climate change, and regional conflicts.
  • Technological & Societal Shifts: Urbanization, population growth, and rapid advances in digital health and Artificial Intelligence.
Q3 • Demographics
According to background data in the policy document, how did average life expectancy in Ethiopia change between 1995 (1987 E.C.) and 2023 (2015 E.C.)?

Answer: Average life expectancy at birth rose significantly from 49 years in 1995 (1987 E.C.) to 66.7 years in 2023 (2015 E.C.), demonstrating substantial gains driven by the expansion of primary healthcare and child survival programs.

Q4 • Constitutional Mandates
Which specific articles of the Constitution of the FDRE establish the state's obligation to protect and improve public health?

Answer:

  • Article 89(8): Mandates that the government holds the obligation to allocate resources to protect and improve public health, safety, and general welfare.
  • Article 90(1): Directs state policies to ensure that all Ethiopians have access to public health services, clean water, and basic social amenities within available national resources.

Section 2: Policy Principles, Vision & Objectives (መبادዎች፣ ራዕይ እና ዓላማዎች)

Q5 • Core Principles
List and explain the seven core guiding principles upon which the April 2024 Health Policy is founded.

Answer: The seven guiding principles are:

  1. Equity & Inclusion: Uncompromised access for all, eliminating geographic, financial, and social barriers.
  2. Quality & Safety: Standardized, evidence-based, safe, and compassionate clinical care.
  3. Sustainability & Resilience: Domestic resource expansion and emergency preparedness.
  4. Evidence-Based Decision Making: Relying on health metrics, research, and data systems.
  5. Transparency & Accountability: Ethical governance across administrative and facility tiers.
  6. Multisectoral Collaboration: Active partnership with non-health sectors ("Health in All Policies").
  7. Professional Ethics & Integrity: High workforce standards, respect, and patient dignity.
Q6 • National Vision
What is the official vision statement defined in the April 2024 Policy document?

Answer: "To see a healthy, productive, and prosperous society through equitable, high-quality, and resilient healthcare services."

Q7 • Strategic Objectives
What are the primary overarching objectives outlined to achieve Universal Health Coverage (UHC) and protect citizens from financial distress?

Answer:

  • Achieve Universal Health Coverage (UHC) by guaranteeing access to an essential package of quality health services.
  • Ensure financial protection through expanded insurance mechanisms (CBHI/SHI) and a dedicated National Health Fund to minimize direct out-of-pocket medical expenses.
  • Build a resilient health system capable of anticipating, preventing, responding to, and recovering from public health risks and emergencies.

Section 3: Priority Policy Directions (የፖሊሲ ዋና ዋና ጉዳዮች እና አቅጣጫዎች)

Q8 • PHC & Vulnerable Groups
How does the policy adapt the Health Extension Program (HEP) across context settings, and what provisions exist for vulnerable groups?

Answer:

  • Tailored HEP Models: Adapts second-generation HEP to specific operational realities in Agrarian, Pastoralist/Semi-pastoralist (mobile health units), and Urban settings (eCHIS digital reporting, NCD screening).
  • Special Needs & Equity: Explicit directives mandate tailored infrastructure, specialized communication tools, and targeted subsidies for persons with disabilities, elderly citizens, refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and vulnerable women/children.
Q9 • Specialized Care & Medical Tourism
What policy directions are set for specialized care, organ transplantation, ICU capacity expansion, and positioning Ethiopia as a regional medical tourism hub?

Answer:

  • Strengthen tertiary centers for sub-specialties including oncology, cardiology, neurosurgery, organ transplantation, and advanced ICU care.
  • Establish legislative and ethical frameworks for human organ/tissue donation and transplantation.
  • Accredit specialized public and private centers to international quality standards to retain domestic healthcare spending and position Ethiopia as a regional hub for medical tourism in East Africa.
Q10 • "One Health" & Emergencies
How does the policy define the "One Health" strategy regarding zoonotic risks, environmental hazards, and border screening?

Answer:

  • One Health Framework: Mandates collaborative, cross-sectoral surveillance and mitigation for zoonotic outbreaks, antimicrobial resistance (AMR), and food/environmental safety between human, animal, and environmental health sectors.
  • Border Screening: Mandatory screening, quarantine protocols, and surveillance at national borders, international airports, and ports of entry to manage cross-border disease transmission.
Q11 • Digital Health & AI
What specific applications of Artificial Intelligence (AI) are mandated in the policy regarding diagnostic imaging, predictive modeling, and remote care?

Answer: The policy explicitly integrates AI technology for:

  • Diagnostic imaging interpretation (radiology, pathology, screening).
  • Predictive epidemiological modeling for epidemic forecasting and resource allocation.
  • Automated patient electronic medical record (EMR) analysis and clinical decision support.
  • Telemedicine and remote specialist consultation expansion across underserved regions.
Q12 • Community Governance
In what ways does the policy guarantee community representation and women’s empowerment in facility management?

Answer:

  • Mandates community board membership and user representation in primary and secondary hospital governing boards.
  • Enforces structural participation of women in health development teams, facility boards, and local healthcare decision-making bodies.
Q13 • Financing & SBFR
How does the policy address reducing high out-of-pocket medical expenditure, and what are directives concerning the National Health Fund and SBFR?

Answer:

  • National Health Fund (የጤና ፈንድ): Establishes a dedicated national fund supported by government allocation, earmarked health taxes (sin taxes on tobacco/alcohol/sugary beverages), and development partners.
  • SBFR (Special Pharmacy / Revenue Retention): Legally protects health facilities' right to retain internally generated revenue from service fees to procure pharmaceuticals, maintain medical equipment, and improve facility quality.
  • CBHI / SHI Expansion: Scaling up Community-Based Health Insurance and Social Health Insurance to reach universal coverage.
Q14 • Traditional Medicine
What frameworks are established for registration, scientific validation, safety screening, intellectual property, and integration of traditional medicine?

Answer:

  • Establishes formal regulatory pathways under EFDA for licensing traditional practitioners and registering herbal remedies.
  • Requires rigorous laboratory screening and clinical research to scientifically validate efficacy while eliminating harmful practices.
  • Protects traditional practitioners' Intellectual Property (IP) rights and indigenous knowledge regarding medicinal flora.
  • Fosters formal integration of safe traditional remedies into modern health delivery settings.
Q15 • Food Safety & Nutrition
What multisectoral strategies are outlined to ensure food safety from "farm to table" and prevent multi-age malnutrition?

Answer:

  • Integrates food safety enforcement across production, transport, processing, and retail stages ("farm to table").
  • Strengthens national food fortification regulations (e.g., salt iodization, flour fortification).
  • Promotes multisectoral nutrition actions targeting the first 1,000 days of life, adolescent girls, and pregnant women to eradicate stunting and wasting.

Section 4: Key Result Areas / KRAs (ቁልፍ የውጤት መስኮች)

Q16 • Key Result Areas
Detail the six Key Result Areas (KRAs) established by the policy to measure national health performance.

Answer: The policy measures overall health transformation against 6 core KRAs:

  1. Citizen Health Improvement & Mortality Reduction: Lowering maternal, neonatal, infant, and overall premature mortality.
  2. Quality & Equitable Health Infrastructure: Modernizing, equipping, and expanding healthcare facilities across urban and rural zones.
  3. Accessible & Quality Medical Supplies & Equipment: Achieving supply security for essential medicines, vaccines, and diagnostic tools.
  4. Sustainable Financial Capacity Development: Expanding health insurance and domestic financing mechanisms.
  5. Strong Sectoral Implementation Capacity: Developing a competent, ethical workforce and robust health information systems.
  6. Client / Patient Satisfaction: Ensuring high client satisfaction through respectful, compassionate, and transparent care.

Section 5: Implementation Strategies & Inter-Ministerial Roles (የፖሊሲው ማስፈፀሚያ ስትራቴጂዎች)

Q17 • Inter-Ministerial Mandates
According to Section 7 of the policy, describe the explicit statutory health-related mandates assigned across government ministries.

Answer:

  • MInT: Digital health infrastructure, connectivity, and technological innovation.
  • MoE: Health education in school curricula, school feeding programs, and workforce development in teaching hospitals.
  • MoWSA: Protecting vulnerable groups, gender equality in health, and health services for refugees/IDPs.
  • MoA: Agricultural food security, chemical/pesticide safety, and zoonotic control under One Health.
  • Transport & Logistics: Road safety, emission controls, and emergency transportation links.
  • Water & Energy / Urban Infrastructure: Safe drinking water, sanitation facilities, power grid stability for health centers, and physical facility standards.
Q18 • Legislative Directives
Which existing proclamations are slated for amendment, and what new proclamations/directives are mandated under this policy?

Answer:

  • Amendments: Revision of Food and Medicine Administration Proclamation No. 1112/2019, insurance laws, and health regulatory directives.
  • New Enactments: Formulating specialized proclamations for Health Service Governance, Emergency Preparedness and Public Health Control, Organ Transplantation Regulation, and Dangerous Pathogen Control.
Q19 • Monitoring & Evaluation
How does the policy establish monitoring, evaluation, and periodic policy review mechanisms across administrative tiers?

Answer:

  • Establishes structured quarterly and annual performance reviews using standardized KPI frameworks across federal, regional (RHB), zonal, and district (Woreda) health offices.
  • Mandates mid-term policy reviews and a comprehensive 10-year policy evaluation cycle to adapt strategies to evolving outcomes.

የኢትዮጵያ ፌዴራላዊ ዲሞክራሲያዊ ሪፐብሊክ የጤና ፖሊሲ (ሚያዚያ 2016 ዓ.ም / April 2024)

1. መግቢያ (Preamble)

ሀገራችን ከድህነት ወደ ብልጽግና በሚደረገው ጉዞ ሰብዓዊ ልማትን ማዕከል በማድረግ ሶስት የጤና ፖሊሲዎችን ተግበራለች፦

  1. የ1960ዎቹ ፖሊሲ፦ ፈንጣጣን ማጥፋትና የወባ ሞትን መካከለኛ ደረጃ መቀነስ ተችሏል።
  2. የ1970ዎቹ አጋማሽ ፖሊሲ፦ የመጀመሪያ ደረጃ ጤና ክብካቤ እና የክትባት መርሃ-ግብር የተስፋፋበት ነበር።
  3. የ1986 ዓ.ም ፖሊሲ፦ የጤና ኤክስቴንሽን ፕሮግራምን በመዘርጋት ተላላፊ በሽታዎችን መከላከልና የእናቶች/ህጻናት ጤና ማሻሻል አስችሏል። የአማካይ ዕድሜ ጣሪያ ከ49 ዓመት (1987 ዓ.ም) ወደ 66.7 ዓመት (2015 ዓ.ም) አድጓል።

የፖሊሲው ክለሳ አስፈላጊነት፦ ከስነ-ህዝብ እድገት፣ ከከተሞች መስፋፋት፣ ከተላላፊና ተላላፊ ካልሆኑ በሽታዎች ጭማሪ፣ ከከፍተኛ ሕክምና ወጪ እና ጥራትና ፍትሃዊነት ክፍተቶች በመነሳት ፖሊሲውን ማሻሻል አስፈላጊ ሆኗል።

2. የጤና ፖሊሲ መነሻዎች (Foundations of the Health Policy)

  • ሕገ-መንግስታዊ እና መብት መሠረት፦ በኢ.ፌ.ዴ.ሪ ሕገ-መንግስት አንቀጽ 89 (8) እና 90 (1) መሠረት የመንግስት ኃላፊነትና የአለም አቀፍ ሰብዓዊ መብቶች አካል መሆኑ።
  • የጤና ስጋቶችና ፈተናዎች፦ የስነ-ህዝብ ስብጥር ለውጥ፣ ተላላፊና ተላላፊ ያልሆኑ በሽታዎች፣ የህብረተሰብ ጤና አደጋዎች፣ የሰው ኃይልና የዲጂታል ቴክኖሎጂ ክፍተቶች፣ እና ከኪስ የሚከፈል ከፍተኛ የህክምና ወጪ።
  • ግቦች፦ ሁሉን አቀፍ የጤና ሽፋን (UHC) ማረጋገጥ፣ ጠንካራና የማይበገር የጤና ስርዓት መገንባት።

3. የፖሊሲው አስፈላጊነት፣ መርሆዎች፣ ራዕይ እና ዓላማዎች

3.1 መርሆዎች (Guiding Principles)

  • ፍትሃዊነት እና አካታችነት፦ ደህንነቱ የተጠበቀ፣ ውጤታማ እና ዋጋው ተመጣጣኝ አገልግሎት ማቅረብ።
  • ጥራት እና ልህቀት፦ የታካሚና ባለሙያ ደህንነት የተጠበቀ የላቀ አገልግሎት።
  • ዘላቂነትና ራስን መቻል፦ አስተማማኝ አገራዊ የጤና ፋይናንስ።
  • መረጃ ላይ ያተኮረ ውሳኔ አሰጣጥ፦ በጥናትና መደበኛ መረጃ መመራት።
  • ግልጽነትና ተጠያቂነት፦ በየደረጃው ያለ የአመራር ስርዓት።
  • ዘርፈ ብዙ ትብብር፦ ከልዩ ልዩ ባለድርሻ አካላት ጋር መስራት።
  • ሙያዊ ስነ-ምግባር የተላበሰ አገልግሎት፦ ሩህሩህና አክባሪ የሰው ኃይል።

3.2 ራዕይ (Vision)

"ጤናማ፣ ምርታማና የበለጸገ ኅብረተሰብ ተፈጥሮ ማየት።"

3.3 ዋና ዋና ዓላማዎች (Main Objectives)

  • ጤናን በማበልጸግና በሽታን በመከላከል ሁሉን አቀፍ የጤና ሽፋን ማሳደግ።
  • ዜጎች ለፋይናንስ ቀውስ ሳይዳረጉ አገልግሎት የሚያገኙበት ዘላቂ የጤና ፋይናንስ መዘርጋት።
  • የመከላከል ዕድል ያላቸውን የእናቶች፣ የህጻናትና የጨቅላ ህጻናት ህመምና ሞት መቀነስ።
  • በተላላፊ፣ ተላላፊ ባልሆኑ፣ በአእምሮ ጤና እና በአደጋዎች የሚደርሱ ህመሞችና ሞትን መቀነስ።
  • ህዝብ-ተኮርና ተዓማኒ የጤና ስርዓት መፍጠር።

4. የፖሊሲ ዋና ዋና ጉዳዮች እና አቅጣጫዎች (Key Policy Issues & Directions)

  1. የጤና አገልግሎት ጥራት፣ ፍትሐዊነትና ተደራሽነት፦ የመጀመሪያ ደረጃ ጤና ክብካቤን ማስፋፋት፣ የቤተሰብ ህክምና፣ የእናቶችና ህጻናት ጤና፣ የአካል ጉዳተኞችና አረጋውያን ልዩ ፍላጎት ማሟላት፣ የጽኑ ህክምና/ቴርሻሪ አገልግሎትን ማስፋፋትና ሜዲካል ቱሪዝምን ማጠናከር።
  2. የማህበረሰብ ተሳትፎ እና ባለቤትነት፦ የጤና ኤክስቴንሽን ፕሮግራምን ማጠናከር፣ ሴቶችንና ወጣቶችን ማሳተፍ፣ በማህበረሰብ ቁጥጥር ግልጽነትና ተጠያቂነት ማረጋገጥ።
  3. የህብረተሰብ ጤና አደጋዎች ቁጥጥርና ምላሽ፦ የቅድመ ማስጠንቀቂያ ስርዓት፣ የ"አንድ ጤና" (One Health) ስትራቴጂ ትግበራ፣ የድንበር አካባቢና የተጓዦች ጤና ቁጥጥር።
  4. የግል እና የአካባቢ ጤና አጠባበቅ፦ ደረቅና ፈሳሽ ቆሻሻ አወጋገድን ማሻሻል፣ የውሃ ጥራት ቁጥጥር፣ አየር ንብረት ለውጥን የሚቋቋም የጤና ስርዓት መገንባት።
  5. የምግብ ደህንነት እና ስርዓተ ምግብ፦ ከእርሻ እስከ ገበታ የምግብ ደህንነት ማረጋገጥ፣ የምግብ አቅርቦትና ንጥረ-ምግብ ይዞታ ማሻሻል።
  6. የጤና ዘርፍ የመፈጸምና የማስፈጸም አቅም፦
    • የሰው ሀብት ልማት፦ የሙያ ስብጥር ማስተካከል፣ የብቃት ምዘና (licensing)፣ ተከታታይ ሙያዊ ስልጠና።
    • ጥናትና ምርምር፦ ችግር ፈች ምርምሮች፣ የሀገር ውስጥ መድኃኒትና ግብአት ምርምር፣ የምርምር አማካሪ ካውንስልና የባዮሜዲካል ጥናት ማጠናከር።
    • ዲጂታል ቴክኖሎጂና መረጃ፦ አርቴፊሻል ኢንተለጀንስ (AI)፣ ቴሌ-ጤና (Telehealth)፣ የልደትና ሞት ምዝገባ ማጠናከር።
    • ቁጥጥር ስርዓት፦ ነጻና ገለልተኛ ተቆጣጣሪ ተቋም፣ የህገ-ወጥ መድኃኒትና አደንዛዥ ዕፆች ቁጥጥር።
    • አመራርና አስተዳደር፦ ብቃት መሠረት ያደረገ የአመራር ምደባ፣ የስርዓተ-ጾታ ስብጥር።
  7. የመድኃኒት፣ የህክምና መሳሪያዎችና ግብዓት አቅርቦት፦ የሀገር ውስጥ አምራቾችን ማጠናከር፣ የመሰረታዊ መድኃኒቶች አቅርቦት ማረጋገጥ፣ ጥገናና አጠቃቀም ቁጥጥር።
  8. የጤና ፋይናንሲንግ፦ የጤና መድህን (CBHI/SHI) ማስፋፋት፣ የጤና ፈንድ ማቋቋም፣ የመንግስትና የግል አጋርነት (PPP) ማጠናከር።
  9. የሀገር በቀል እውቀትና ባህል ህክምና፦ የባህል ህክምናን ከዘመናዊ ህክምና ጋር ማቀናጀት፣ ጥናትና ምርምር ማካሄድ።
  10. የጤና ዘርፈ ብዙ ትብብር፦ ከሌሎች ሚኒስቴር መስሪያ ቤቶች፣ ከልማት አጋሮች እና ከሲቪክ ማህበራት ጋር የተቀናጀ ምላሽ መስጠት።

Overview: The April 2024 (ሚያዚያ 2016 ዓ.ም) Health Policy

The revised Health Policy of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia was officially enacted in April 2024 (ሚያዚያ 2016 ዓ.ም) by the Council of Ministers. Replacing the 1993 (1986 E.C.) policy framework, it aligns national healthcare with current demographic, epidemiological, and technological shifts. The policy emphasizes a resilient health system, universal health coverage (UHC), domestic health financing, artificial intelligence in digital health, and institutionalized traditional medicine.

1960s E.C. (1st Generation)
1. Early Public Health & Smallpox Eradication Era

Focused on infectious disease campaigns, successfully achieving smallpox eradication and controlling major epidemic outbreaks.

Mid-1970s E.C. (2nd Generation)
2. Primary Health Care (PHC) & Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI)

Initiated formal Primary Health Care strategies and expanded child immunization across regions.

1993 / 1986 E.C. (3rd Generation)
3. Decentralization & Health Extension Program (HEP)

Established preventive healthcare focus, 3-tier delivery systems, and the Health Extension Program. Average life expectancy increased from 49 years (1995) to 66.7 years (2023).

April 2024 / ሚያዚያ 2016 ዓ.ም (4th Generation Enacted)
4. Comprehensive FDRE Health Policy & Universal Health Coverage

Formally enacted by the Council of Ministers. Centers on high-quality tertiary care, resilient health emergency systems, domestic financing/health funds, digital health/AI integration, and multisectoral "One Health" governance.

1. Primary Health Care & Equitable Access

  • Second-Generation HEP: Upskilling HEWs, expanding eCHIS electronic registry, and tailoring care models for Agrarian, Pastoralist/Semi-pastoralist, and Urban settings.
  • Special Needs & Inclusivity: Specialized access for persons with disabilities, elderly citizens, refugees, and vulnerable populations without discrimination based on geography, gender, or status.
  • Integrated Home & Family Care: Strengthening home-based and family-centered clinical care delivery networks.

2. Advanced Tertiary Care, Sub-Specialization & Medical Tourism

  • Specialty Expansion: Strengthening oncology, cardiology, neurosurgery, organ transplantation, and intensive care units (ICU).
  • Medical Tourism: Developing domestic technical excellence to curb overseas medical flight and establish Ethiopia as a regional medical tourism hub.
  • Quality Certification: Accredit public and private tertiary centers to international and national standards.

3. Digital Health, AI & Knowledge Management

  • Artificial Intelligence (AI): Integrating AI for diagnostic imaging/pathology interpretation, predictive health modeling, digital patient record analysis, and supporting remote telemedicine.
  • Data Governance & Legal Framework: Establishing explicit legal frameworks for health data privacy, confidentiality, and secure information exchange.
  • Vital Statistics: Strengthening nationwide civil registration and vital statistics (CRVS) for births and deaths.

4. Public Health Emergency Management & "One Health"

  • One Health Approach: Collaborative management of zoonotic diseases and environmental risks across human, animal, and environmental domains.
  • Border Screening & Travelers' Health: Mandatory health screening at national borders and ports of entry to manage cross-border epidemic threats.
  • Surveillance & Laboratory Surge: Strengthening real-time surveillance under EPHI and expanding diagnostic laboratory capacity.

1. Epidemiological & Demographic Transition

Surging double burden of disease: Non-Communicable Diseases (cardiovascular, diabetes, cancers, mental health disorders) alongside endemic communicable diseases and traumatic injuries.

2. High Out-of-Pocket Expenditure & Financial Risk

Rising out-of-pocket medical expenses driving citizens into financial crises, necessitating dedicated National Health Funds, integrated health insurance, and sustainable domestic revenue generation.

3. Health Security & Emerging Global Threats

Lessons from COVID-19, regional conflicts, climate-induced health crises, and emerging epidemics requiring resilient disaster management structures.

4. Core Policy Guiding Principles (April 2024 Policy)

  • Equity & Inclusivity: Safe, effective, and affordable care for all citizens.
  • Quality & Excellence: High-standard, patient-centered clinical care.
  • Sustainability & Self-Reliance: Strong domestic health financing and resource independence.
  • Evidence-Based Decisions: Utilizing routine data and health research for strategic policymaking.
  • Transparency & Accountability: Ethical governance across all health system administrative tiers.
  • Multisectoral Collaboration: Coordinated action across government ministries and private actors.
  • Professional Ethics: Compassionate, respectful, confidential, and caring health workforce.

Institutional reform frameworks, legislative directives, and operational guidelines:

EHSIG / EHSTG — Hospital Service Improvement Guidelines ▼

Overview: Standardizes management across Primary, General, and Comprehensive Specialized Hospitals.

  • Clinical Operations: Standardizing triage, ICU, morning handovers, and discharge summaries.
  • APTS & Pharmacy: Auditable Pharmaceutical Transactions and Services ensuring financial transparency and supply chain control.
Sustainable Health Financing & Domestic Health Fund ▼

Overview: Establishes a dedicated National Health Fund (የጤና ፈንድ) and expands Community-Based Health Insurance (CBHI) and Social Health Insurance (SHI).

  • Internal Revenue Retention: Facilities legally retain internal revenue (SBFR) to procure essential inputs, maintain equipment, and incentivize workforce performance.
  • Local Manufacturing Incentives: Targeted incentives for local pharmaceutical, medical device, and vaccine production to achieve supply security.
Multisectoral Governance & Ministry Responsibilities ▼

Overview: Section 7 of the 2024 Policy outlines specific statutory mandates across ministries:

  • Ministry of Innovation & Technology (MInT): Digital health infrastructure, ICT integration, and technological research support.
  • Ministry of Education (MoE): Health curricula integration, school feeding, health clubs, and high-quality health workforce training in teaching hospitals.
  • Ministry of Women & Social Affairs (MoWSA): Health empowerment for women/youth, child protection, and health access for refugees and vulnerable groups.
  • Ministry of Water & Energy / Urban & Infrastructure: Safe drinking water, hygiene infrastructure, power supply, and health facility physical standards.
  • Ministry of Agriculture: Food security, safe agricultural practice, and zoonotic outbreak control under One Health.
  • Ministry of Transport & Logistics: Mitigating traffic injuries, vehicle emissions control, and priority transport for medical supplies.
EHAQ / CASH / EBC Quality Initiatives ▼

EHAQ: Cluster-based hospital mentorship and quality scoring.

CASH: Clean and Safe Health Facility Initiative focusing on IPC and sanitation.

EBC: Evidence-Based Care driving compliance with updated Standard Treatment Guidelines (STGs).

Regulatory Oversight & Re-licensing

Enforced by autonomous national regulatory bodies (EFDA) and regional authorities.

  • Mandatory Continuous Professional Development (CPD) credit points and standard competency assessments for medical re-licensing.
  • Surveillance and enforcement against substandard, counterfeit, and illegal medicines or devices.

Indigenous Knowledge & Traditional Medicine Integration

Formally standardizes and institutionalizes Ethiopian traditional medicine.

  • Institutional Integration: Integration of safe, verified traditional medical practices alongside modern health services.
  • Registration & Intellectual Property: Establishing national registries for indigenous medical knowledge, plant conservation, and protecting practitioner intellectual property.
  • Safety & Research: Mandatory scientific screening to eliminate harmful practices while validating therapeutic efficacy in bio-prospecting research.

Medical Ethics, Liability & Professional Committees

Professional Ethics and Medico-Legal Review Committees evaluate cases against national STGs to ensure professional accountability, protect patient rights, and address clinical negligence.
